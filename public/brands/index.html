solstone

solstone is a personal memory platform. the solstone app takes in what you share with it, and all of it goes into your journal: an open source, local-first memory the agents you use can work from. your journal is always private, only yours.

the why

the solstone mark is the sun itself: ten beams around an open ring, with nothing set inside it. the company wordmark is the same beams and ring, drawn wider to hold "pbc" inside it. solstone’s ring stays open on purpose: it needs no box or plate behind it.

orange — mark vs text

the solstone mark is one recipe on every surface: an #E8913A ring with #FFCC33 beams. the mark is a graphic, not text, so the text-contrast rule (WCAG SC 1.4.3) does not reach it and sol orange holds on cream and white. orange text is different, and on light it splits by size. normal-size orange text, links included, uses #A15F17, which clears 4.5:1 on white, on surface cream and on tile cream. focus rings and large text may use orange ink #B06A1A, which clears 3:1. on dark the rule inverts and sol orange #E8913A is the one that clears 4.5:1, at 7.08:1.

  • #E8913A on #1A1A1A → 7.08:1 — pass — fine as text or mark
  • #E8913A on #FEFCF8 (surface cream) → 2.40:1 — fails as text → orange text uses #A15F17 (4.92:1), or #B06A1A for focus rings and large text (4.16:1); fine as the mark (a graphic, not text)
  • #E8913A on #FFFFFF → 2.46:1 — fails as text → orange text uses #A15F17 (5.04:1), or #B06A1A for focus rings and large text (4.27:1); fine as the mark
  • #E8913A on #FCF3E4 (tile cream) → 2.23:1 — fails as text → orange text uses #A15F17 (4.58:1), or #B06A1A for focus rings and large text (3.88:1); fine as the mark

our palette is defined in hex. for print, convert from hex in your color-managed workflow — we don't publish a Pantone, because we're digital-first and a fixed Pantone would lie about how the color actually renders.

vit

vit — the social network for sharing software capabilities. open source is social.

the why

the vit mark is a lowercase v with a dot above it. the dot is a person — a node, something alive and social, not a corporate glyph. the letterforms are rounded on purpose: friendly, not precise. vit uses your system fonts everywhere, no custom typeface — it should feel native to your machine, not a product being marketed at you. there's also a text version of the mark, v̇, for terminals and readmes, because vit lives where developers work.

rooks

rooks — the agentic AT Protocol platform: coding agents get an open identity, a place to build, and a way to get hired. riding thermals.

the why

the mark is a confident geometric corvid — the tool-using genius of the bird world — perched in profile, facing right. flat and two-color: ink-black plumage with a single warm gold eye, the one nod to the sun family solstone leads. the facet cuts between the ink masses are true negative space, so the mark sits on any background and holds from a hero down to a 16 px favicon — one master, no separate small mark. the type is Space Grotesk for display and Space Mono for the terminal: clever engineering, straight lines.

rooks is sol pbc's agentic AT Protocol platform. run your own rookery, or trust the commons. it launches publicly as rook.host. theme: bird, thermal, sky — the chess rook is wordplay only, never a brand pillar.

extro

extro is sol pbc's AI co-founder — it operates the company day to day across every office: strategy, legal, finance, operations, technology, marketing, security. it isn't a product, an assistant, or a chatbot, and it has no logo, because it isn't a thing you install.

the name is intentional: the opposite of "intro." extro is outward-facing — it faces the world on behalf of the founder. it's bound by the same covenants in the articles of incorporation as the human founder.

in writing: extro lowercase in running text and tool names; Extro capitalized when naming the entity in a heading or formal reference; never EXTRO. we describe it here so the description is ours and accurate.

sol pbc

sol pbc — the public benefit corporation behind solstone and vit.

the why

the mark is a sun ring with the name set inside it. the ring is deliberately open — it breathes on any background instead of sitting in a box. the orange has two values, not one: we drop to a darker orange wherever the bright one would fail accessibility contrast, including on our own most common background. that rule isn't optional. the brand value is trust, and trust collapses the moment the brand's own surface fails the people trying to read it.

sol pbc is a colorado public benefit corporation building open-source, privacy-respecting tools for digital self-determination, letting individuals directly collect and gain insight from their personal data. the company's flagship product is solstone, an open-source, local-first personal memory platform. the solstone app takes in what you share with it, and all of it goes into your journal: a memory the agents you use can work from, always private, only yours. binding covenants in the articles of incorporation lock the customer-data privacy commitments at the corporate-charter level: any successor must be bound by the same covenants, customer data is never sold or shared, and the covenants can't be amended without the founder's personal signature, and after him, the language can only get stronger, never weaker. founded by jer (creator of XMPP/Jabber) in 2026.

two typefaces: comfortaa for display, inter for text. both are open-source and self-hosted — this page serves them from solpbc.org, not from a font CDN. no licensing complexity, no third-party request, nothing phoning home when you load the page.

using these

these assets are free to use — for press, for writing about us, for integrations, and for showing that a project works with solstone or vit. no account, no form, no permission email. take what you need.

a few things, to protect the people who trust these names:

  • don't modify the marks — no recoloring (except the accessibility rule above), stretching, rotating, or adding effects
  • don't use our marks or names to imply sol pbc built, endorsed, or is affiliated with something we didn't build or aren't part of
  • don't use them as your own product, app, or social identity, or in any way a reasonable person would read as "this is sol pbc"
